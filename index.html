About Us

Why the Energy Efficiency Club Exists

Established under the Department of Electrical & Electronics Engineering at Sri Sairam College of Engineering, our mission is to build practical engineering competence in energy conservation, clean generation, and responsible power utilization.

We believe energy conservation is not simply an abstract environmental ideal — it is an active engineering discipline.

The Energy Efficiency Club promotes responsible use of electrical energy and sustainable engineering practices across campus and society. Rather than solely discussing green technology in theory, our student members conduct real electrical load analyses, identify phantom power losses, evaluate appliance energy efficiency ratings, and build functional prototypes that make conservation visible and actionable.

From campus energy audits and state symposium presentations to field exposures at institutions like the Mahatma Gandhi Institute of Rural Energy & Development (MGIRED), we empower engineering students to lead sustainable change.

Strategic Direction

Our Vision

A campus and society that actively practises efficient, responsible, and sustainable use of electrical energy — accelerated through awareness, student innovation, and practical technology deployment.

Active Commitment

Our Mission

Promote continuous awareness, hands-on engineering innovation, and practical implementation of energy-efficient, clean-energy technologies among students, faculty, and the wider community.

Core Operational Objectives

  • ✓ Promote efficient and responsible use of electrical energy across campus facilities.
  • ✓ Build critical awareness on polluting vs. non-polluting electrical and electronic devices.
  • ✓ Analyse and benchmark appliance power consumption using standard star ratings.
  • ✓ Push the active adoption of solar, bio-energy, and clean decentralized energy solutions.
  • ✓ Identify and cut everyday electrical losses in laboratory, hostel, and campus infrastructure.
Core Domains

Our Key Focus Areas

Our structured initiatives bridge theoretical electrical engineering with applied environmental conservation across six critical operational areas.

Energy Auditing

Comprehensive load monitoring across campus departments, identifying phantom standby power, and recommending corrective measures to minimize peak demand.

Renewable Energy

Exploring photovoltaic arrays, solar-thermal conversion, bio-mass digestors, and technical modeling using MATLAB/Simulink for microgrid reliability.

Energy Conservation

Cultivating institutional habits that reduce electrical wastage, promoting BEE star-label literacy, and optimizing appliance duty cycles.

Sustainable Technology

Engineering autonomous inspection rovers, automated smart metering interfaces, and sensor modules to safeguard electrical transmission lines.

Environmental Awareness

Student-led workshops, National Energy Conservation Day celebrations, technical bootcamps, and creative logo & poster competitions.

E-Waste Management

"Junk to Energy" upcycling drives that salvage discarded capacitors, coils, and power components from scrap to build working educational testbenches.

Symposium Demonstration

Smart Cable Rover

Overhead & Underground Cable Inspection Robot

An eco-friendly, dual-pulley robotic rover designed and fabricated by club members. Built to ride overhead and distribution conductors to detect physical line sagging, thermal degradation, and surface faults before catastrophic electrical failure occurs — proof of what dedicated student club engineering can achieve.

Campus Audit Program

Departmental Wattage & Standby Power Analysis

Students profile lab computers, lighting fixtures, and air-conditioning units to determine baseline kilowatt-hour consumption and calculate cumulative carbon emission reductions from automated shutdown protocols.

Circularity Initiative

"Junk to Energy" Hardware Upcycling

E-waste collection campaigns where non-functional electronic waste and obsolete motors are disassembled, tested, and repurposed into benchtop demonstration generators and instructional models for junior students.
